Smith College Dining Spending Analysis

Overview

In this visual analytic system, we will explore how Smith sources the food they provide in their dining halls using data collected by the CEEDS department (Center for the Environment, Ecological Design, and Sustainability). Below we have 3 visualization to help display this distribution of funds.

Smith College Food Spending: Local vs Non-Local

This bar chart compares the total dining spending at Smith College on local food suppliers versus non-local suppliers. The "local" category includes all food purchased from suppliers based in the same region as Smith. The "non-local" category includes all food purchased from suppliers outside of that region. By visualizing this data, we can quickly see the proportion of dining funds that support local food systems compared to those that do not, which can inform discussions about sustainability, community impact, and opportunities for increasing local sourcing in the future.

Research Question

How does the total dining spending at Smith College compare between local and non-local food suppliers?

Spending Breakdown

These pie charts provide a deeper breakdown of the dining spending at Smith College. The first chart compares the total spending on "real" food (minimally processed, whole foods) versus "not real" food (highly processed, convenience foods). The second chart breaks down the "real" food spending into specific categories such as Sustainable, Humane, Fair, Local. This analysis helps to understand not only how much is being spent on local vs. non-local suppliers but also the quality and type of food being purchased, which can have implications for health, sustainability, and community support.

Research Question

How does the dining spending at Smith College break down between real and not real food, and what are the specific categories of real food being purchased?

Dining Fund Distribution Analysis

This Sankey diagram explores how dining funds are distributed among food suppliers used by Smith College, with a focus on the split between local and non-local vendors.

The visualization traces the flow of total dining budget spending into local vs. non-local categories, then further breaks it down into plant-based and non-plant-based foods, and finally into individual distributors.

The goal of this analysis is to better understand how much of the dining budget supports local suppliers and to identify opportunities where Smith College could potentially increase spending with local vendors if aligned with sustainability, freshness, and community impact goals.

Research Question

How are dining funds distributed between local and non-local suppliers at Smith College, and what opportunities exist to shift spending toward local food systems?
